This small text is a software test fixture written for the MegaFMM experiment suite.
It is not a language modeling benchmark dataset. Results on this text establish
that tokenization, backend substitution, likelihood evaluation, and provenance
recording work together. They do not establish general language model quality.

A matrix multiplication routine receives two arrays and computes their product.
An application may call the routine many times, with different shapes and values.
The experiment records both the time spent and the quality of the final result.
Changing a numerical backend can change rounding, so speed and accuracy are
measured independently.

Scientific calculations often build matrices from physical models. Neural
networks build matrices from observations, weights, gradients, and intermediate
features. In both cases, an experiment needs a clear input, a reproducible
reference, and a stated success criterion.

An isolated benchmark starts each invocation from the same initial state.
An iterative solver checks its residual before declaring convergence. A model
evaluation computes likelihood on a fixed sequence of tokens. These operations
serve different purposes and should retain distinct labels in the final report.

The experiment suite preserves slow results and failed quality checks. A faster
calculation is useful when its result meets the requirements of the application.
